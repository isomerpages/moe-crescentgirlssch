<p><strong>1950s</strong>
</p>
</td>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p><strong>The Beginning</strong>
</p>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p></p>
</td>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p>In 1955, Alexandra Estate Secondary School started with 117 boys and 53
girls. By the end of the year, the boys were transferred to Pasir Panjang
Secondary.</p>
<p></p>
<p>In 1956, founding Principal, Miss Evelyn Norris, renamed the school Crescent
Girls’ School. The word “Crescent” came from the name of the road, Prince
Charles Crescent, where the school was situated.</p>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p><strong>1960s</strong>
</p>
</td>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p><strong>Building Identity</strong>
</p>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p></p>
</td>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p>Development gained momentum in the 1960s as the school embarked on the
pathway to progress.&nbsp;</p>
<p></p>
<p>In 1967, the Brass Band, under the baton of the band instructor, Mr Lee
Seck Chiang, staged its first Musical Evening at the Singapore Conference
Hall. By 1969, the Brass Band was one of the Top Three brass bands among
secondary schools.</p>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p><strong>1970s</strong>
</p>
</td>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p>Education beyond academic pursuits was the key motivating factor as Crescent
entered a new phase of development in the 1970s.</p>
<p></p>
<p>In 1978, a total of 543 girls, the largest number of candidates in the
school’s history, sat for the Singapore-Cambridge GCE O Level Examination.</p>
<p></p>
<p>In 1979, the junior &amp; senior hockey teams and junior softball team
were the National Champions. The National Cadet Corps also aced the NCC
Annual Drill Competition. The Band converted from a Brass Band to a Symphonic
Band.</p>
<p></p>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p><strong>1980s</strong>
</p>
</td>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p><strong>Growing Community</strong>
</p>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p></p>
</td>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p>Crescent became more than a school — it became a lifelong community of
mentorship, friendship and shared purpose.</p>
<p></p>
<p>In 1980, the Crescent Girls’ School Alumnae Association was established.
In 1986, the first Parents-Teachers Day was held to establish closer rapport
with parents.</p>
<p></p>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p><strong>1990s</strong>
</p>
</td>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p><strong>Renewal &amp; Transformation</strong>
</p>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p></p>
</td>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p>To facilitate rebuilding of the current school, Crescent moved to temporary
premises at Queensway in December 1991.</p>
<p></p>
<p>On 5 December 1993, past and present Crescentians were out in full force
in the MEGA-MOVE as Crescent returned to the new school building at Tanglin
Road.</p>
<p></p>
<p>The official opening of the new and bigger premises with Dr and Mrs Wee
Kim Wee as the distinguished Guests-of-Honour. A Time Capsule was sealed
on 21 July 1995 with plans to open it 25 years later.</p>
<p></p>
<p>In 1996, Crescent was conferred the ‘Autonomous School’ status by the
Ministry of Education and given additional funds for innovative enrichment
programmes.</p>
<p></p>
<p>Information Technology (IT) became a cornerstone in the school’s development
from the mid-1990s. Crescent was one of the IT Demo schools and was also
selected as a FastTrack school.</p>
<p></p>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p><strong>2000s</strong>
</p>
</td>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p><strong>Pioneers of Innovation</strong>
</p>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p></p>
</td>
<td rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p>Crescent was affirmed as a leader in its innovative use of IT and made
an indelible mark on the education landscape.</p>
<p></p>
<p>In 2003, Crescent was recognised as the Centre of Excellence for IT and
Creative Arts. Crescent grew to be a leading arts education hub in the
ASEAN region through innovative pedagogy and collaborative outreach.</p>
<p></p>
<p>On 31 July 2004, the Mobile-Learning@Crescent was launched by Mr Tharman
Shanmugaratnam, then acting Minister for Education. Crescent was also awarded
the Incubator School status for its role at the forefront of IT and research.</p>
<p></p>
<p>In 2007, Crescent was selected as a pioneer school in the FutureSchools@Singapore
programme and a Mentor School in Microsoft Corporation’s Innovative Schools
Programme – a testament to the school’s innovative use of Information and
Communications Technology (ICT) for teaching and learning. Innovations
in curriculum were introduced, with Secondary 1, 2 and 3 Integrated Curriculum
rolled out to students to help them see connections across different subjects,
and with the real world.</p>
